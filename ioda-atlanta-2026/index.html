01 · The Bandit

The routing problem.

We run thousands of routes across a dozen+ providers. Every route can be blocked. Block patterns differ by country, by ISP, by hour.

No human can keep up. So we don't try.

01 · The Bandit

A multi-armed bandit, per ASN.

Every (data center, protocol) combination is an arm. Every connection is a pull. Success is a reward, blocking is the opposite.

The EXP3.S algorithm balances exploration and exploitation, with a shift parameter so good arms that suddenly go bad — the censor caught up — can be re-learned without restart.

Crucially, we learn per (ASN, country): what works for Iranian Mobile may not work for IranCell, may not work for a fiber ISP in Hangzhou.

EXP3.S · γ = 0.20 · α = shift
60s
poll when learning
15m
poll when converged

Confidence is computed from weight entropy: high-entropy ASNs poll fast for more data, converged ones back off.

01 · The Bandit × WATER

A bandit with a
moving action space.

The standard multi-armed bandit assumes a fixed set of arms. In censorship circumvention that assumption breaks the moment the censor ships a new classifier — and again when we ship a counter. The arms themselves are non-stationary, and the catalog of arms keeps growing.

Bandit

Picks the working arm, per region.

EXP3.S handles non-stationary rewards — we re-learn an arm that the censor catches up to without restarting. But the arms it can choose from are still whatever shipped in the binary.

WATER

Adds and replaces arms at runtime.

WebAssembly transports ship as data, not code. A new WATER module rolls out through the config service and shows up in the bandit's catalog within one poll cycle — no app release, no app-store review queue.

When a censor closes a gap, we don't ship a new client. We ship a new arm — and the bandit finds it, for the regions where it works.

Live operational view: bandit.lantern.io

02 · The New VPN

We rebuilt the whole client
on sing-box.

The old Lantern was Lantern's own protocol stack, end to end. Powerful but isolated — every change was ours alone to write, ours alone to keep working.

The new one rides on sing-box, the universal proxy platform — and adds Lantern's protocols as a public library: lantern-box.

Everything we build is now usable by every other circumvention tool. Everything they build is usable by us.

02 · The New VPN · Protocol Diversity

Each protocol is its own
detection problem.

We ship many protocols on purpose. Each one forces the censor to learn it, build a detector for it, deploy that detector at scale. That's expensive. Diversity is the defense.

03 · Unbounded

Volunteer connections
become routes.

A swarm of short-lived residential IPs — provided by volunteers in less censored regions — carries traffic for users in heavily censored ones. The censor can't easily pre-block what doesn't exist yet.

Direct lineage from Flash Proxy (Stanford, 2012) through uProxy through Psiphon's Conduit — the volunteer-relay model at the largest scale we've seen deployed. Same core idea, refined for browser-based deployment and concurrent multiplexing.

Open source: github.com/getlantern/unbounded · Live widget: unbounded.lantern.io

03 · Unbounded · Recently shipped

Every Lantern user
can now be a route.

Until recently, Unbounded was a separate web widget. We've now integrated it directly into the Lantern VPN client across platforms.

Volunteers in safe regions get a one-click "Share My Connection" toggle. Users in blocked regions get residential IPs the censor has no reason to pre-block — because they didn't exist yesterday.

04 · The Corpus · corpus.lantern.io

Knowing the adversary.

The censorship circumvention field is rich — gfw.report, net4people, OONI, CensorBib. But none of it is LLM-queryable. No common schema. No common vocabulary for "censor / detection technique / defense."

We've built a structured, controlled-vocabulary index of every paper that matters, exposed through an MCP server so any AI assistant can compose corpus queries with internal data queries in the same conversation.

A protocol design conversation now starts from what censors actually do, not from what we remember reading.
